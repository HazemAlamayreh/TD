# ATD Academy — Mastery Check Answer Key — Domain 1


## 1.1 Communication

- **1.1.1-M1 — C.** The revised message identifies audience, purpose, timing and a single clear action. The closest distractor adds detail but still fails to clarify the action.

- **1.1.1-M2 — A.** Combining message comprehension with access barriers tests why action did not occur. Opens alone do not prove comprehension.

- **1.1.2-M1 — D.** Paraphrasing and requesting an example checks understanding without prematurely deciding the solution.

- **1.1.2-M2 — B.** An open invitation and thoughtful pause support active listening and concrete evidence. Closed questions risk narrowing the issue too soon.

- **1.1.3-M1 — B.** Executive briefings should lead with a relevant business need, credible evidence and a focused decision.

- **1.1.3-M2 — C.** A compelling value statement links the intervention to the business gap and evidence of impact. Budget fit alone is insufficient.

- **1.1.4-M1 — A.** Ethical persuasion starts with the stakeholder's stated concern and responds with relevant evidence, not social pressure.

- **1.1.4-M2 — D.** A proportionate pilot addresses the real constraint while retaining measurable business-relevant outcomes.

- **1.1.5-M1 — C.** Decision-focused writing aligns evidence and options with the reader's required decision.

- **1.1.5-M2 — A.** A decision-ready status report prioritizes material progress and risks, with enough evidence for the next decision.

- **1.1.6-M1 — D.** A specific open-ended performance question helps test possible causes before selecting an intervention.

- **1.1.6-M2 — B.** Comparing successful and unsuccessful work conditions tests explanations without presuming a motivation problem.

- **1.1.7-M1 — A.** Structured dialogue surfaces relevant perspectives before narrowing options.

- **1.1.7-M2 — C.** A structured participation method distributes opportunity without singling someone out or reinforcing dominance.

- **1.1.8-M1 — D.** The value proposition connects evidence of a specific organizational problem with a decision.

- **1.1.8-M2 — B.** A credible value case makes evidence and assumptions explicit; the closest alternative avoids projection rather than qualifying it.

## 1.2 Emotional Intelligence & Decision Making

- **1.2.1-M1 — C.** High-stakes judgments need job-relevant evidence, validation and fair assessment.

- **1.2.1-M2 — B.** Observed work behavior is not interchangeable with a self-description score.

- **1.2.2-M1 — B.** Regulation supports inquiry and constructive action under social pressure.

- **1.2.2-M2 — C.** Deliberate behavioral practice and feedback turn awareness into improved response patterns.

- **1.2.3-M1 — C.** Uneven opportunity access can distort apparent readiness and must be considered fairly.

- **1.2.3-M2 — B.** Structured decisions reduce the influence of affinity bias.

- **1.2.4-M1 — C.** Contextual evidence and inclusive design are preferable to stereotyping.

- **1.2.4-M2 — B.** Verbal concerns should be clarified instead of overridden by an isolated gesture.

- **1.2.5-M1 — C.** Adaptation requires understanding legitimate constraints without abandoning agreed outcomes.

- **1.2.5-M2 — B.** Self-regulation plus fair group process supports constructive discussion.

- **1.2.6-M1 — C.** Resilience is evidence-responsive, not persistence with an ineffective intervention.

- **1.2.6-M2 — B.** Adaptation keeps the critical evidence and decision quality despite constraints.

- **1.2.7-M1 — C.** A decision matrix is only as sound as the evidence and constraints it reflects.

- **1.2.7-M2 — D.** Structured decision processes balance evidence, accountability, time and participation.

- **1.2.8-M1 — C.** Critical reasoning weighs evidence quality and contextual relevance before causal claims.

- **1.2.8-M2 — A.** Defensible judgments disclose limitations and avoid false precision.

## 1.3 Collaboration & Leadership

- **1.3.1-M1 — A.** A team requires shared, interdependent work, not merely a meeting schedule.

- **1.3.1-M2 — C.** Agreeing shared outcomes resolves the misalignment before adding more coordination processes.

- **1.3.2-M1 — B.** Mapping collaboration bottlenecks helps redistribute work; more meetings can increase overload.

- **1.3.2-M2 — D.** Stakeholder-based communication reduces duplication while maintaining accountability.

- **1.3.3-M1 — A.** A charter establishes responsibilities and decision authority before deadlines can reliably be met.

- **1.3.3-M2 — B.** The issue is misaligned interpretation of shared goals; the closest alternative improves tracking without resolving it.

- **1.3.4-M1 — C.** Explicit shared criteria integrate competing legitimate requirements.

- **1.3.4-M2 — D.** Non-negotiable requirements must be distinguished from preferences before compromise is possible.

- **1.3.5-M1 — A.** Immediate safety calls for prompt protective action; participative conflict resolution can follow.

- **1.3.5-M2 — C.** Recurring interdependent issues favor collaboration around interests and workable constraints.

- **1.3.6-M1 — B.** Clarifying the problem and needs precedes solution generation and helps avoid positional bargaining.

- **1.3.6-M2 — D.** Shared criteria allow trade-offs even when priorities differ.

- **1.3.7-M1 — C.** PDCA begins with understanding the current workflow and root causes.

- **1.3.7-M2 — A.** Improvement cycles must evaluate all critical outcomes, not speed in isolation.

- **1.3.8-M1 — D.** Developmental delegation combines ownership with boundaries and support.

- **1.3.8-M2 — B.** Clear authority boundaries permit independent work within scope and appropriate escalation beyond it.

- **1.3.9-M1 — B.** Effective feedback identifies observed behavior, its effect and a feasible improvement.

- **1.3.9-M2 — C.** Behavioral observation and learner clarification directly test the target improvement; satisfaction alone is less specific.

- **1.3.10-M1 — A.** Situational leadership matches support and direction to work needs.

- **1.3.10-M2 — D.** Task-specific competence, not tenure, determines the appropriate level of direction and support.

## 1.4 Cultural Awareness & Inclusion

- **1.4.1-M1 — A.** Broad cultural frameworks do not establish how an individual or specific team behaves.

- **1.4.1-M2 — B.** Learner consultation avoids stereotyping while preserving shared job outcomes.

- **1.4.2-M1 — D.** Context-aware language and checking understanding reduce unintended barriers.

- **1.4.2-M2 — C.** Inclusive design removes avoidable ambiguity and validates the revised material with the audience.

- **1.4.3-M1 — A.** Sustained practice, dialogue and accountability help translate awareness into behavior.

- **1.4.3-M2 — A.** Sustained intercultural competence requires authentic interaction and reinforcement, not facts alone.

- **1.4.4-M1 — D.** Accessibility is a design responsibility, not an individual deficit.

- **1.4.4-M2 — D.** Accessibility should be designed and tested as a requirement, not provided only after exclusion.

- **1.4.5-M1 — A.** Transparent access criteria can reveal inequities in development opportunities.

- **1.4.5-M2 — B.** An access analysis needs denominators, selection criteria and representation, not only participant feedback.

- **1.4.6-M1 — D.** Outcome and access indicators assess whether initiatives reach and benefit employees.

- **1.4.6-M2 — C.** Inclusion is reflected in equitable access and outcomes; reporting must also protect sensitive data.

## 1.5 Project Management

- **1.5.2-M1 — C.** The sponsor authorizes the project and its high-level direction and resources.

- **1.5.2-M2 — A.** Formal sponsorship and authority enable accountable project initiation.

- **1.5.3-M1 — B.** The charter provides formal authorization and clarifies project purpose.

- **1.5.3-M2 — D.** A charter establishes the decision framework and authorization, not the full instructional detail.

- **1.5.4-M1 — A.** Task dependencies determine sequencing and timing.

- **1.5.4-M2 — B.** Dependencies and ownership make schedule risks visible before they become rework.

- **1.5.5-M1 — D.** Changes need impact analysis and an agreed approval route.

- **1.5.5-M2 — C.** Controlled change requires assessing impacts and explicit authorization rather than informal scope growth.

- **1.5.6-M1 — C.** Formal acceptance precedes operational handover and closeout tasks.

- **1.5.6-M2 — A.** Formal closure includes accepted deliverables, operations transition and documented learning.

## 1.6 Compliance & Ethical Behavior

- **1.6.1-M1 — C.** Integrity requires honest, transparent communication and accurate evidence.

- **1.6.1-M2 — D.** Professional integrity requires truthful reporting and constructive handling of uncertainty.

- **1.6.2-M1 — D.** Disclosure and a documented process protect fairness and trust.

- **1.6.2-M2 — B.** Disclosure and governed conflict management protect the fairness of the decision.

- **1.6.3-M1 — A.** Access should be lawful, purposeful and limited to authorized need.

- **1.6.3-M2 — C.** Minimum necessary access protects privacy while supporting a legitimate decision.

- **1.6.4-M1 — B.** Educational intent does not automatically authorize reuse of copyrighted material.

- **1.6.4-M2 — A.** Attribution and a different format do not automatically grant reproduction rights.

- **1.6.5-M1 — C.** Employment assessments require careful validation and applicable legal review.

- **1.6.5-M2 — D.** High-impact talent decisions must rest on defensible job-related evidence and applicable requirements.

- **1.6.6-M1 — D.** Contract and data controls must be assessed before access is granted.

- **1.6.6-M2 — B.** Contractual duties and data minimization are essential, while properly aggregated insights may still be useful.

- **1.6.7-M1 — A.** Legal duties depend on jurisdiction and current requirements.

- **1.6.7-M2 — C.** Applicable local requirements need verification; a generic disclaimer does not ensure compliance.

## 1.7 Lifelong Learning

- **1.7.1-M1 — B.** Learning agility requires experimentation, reflection and transfer.

- **1.7.1-M2 — A.** Learning agility involves experimentation, feedback and transfer, not merely exposure to content.

- **1.7.2-M1 — A.** Career exploration requires individual insight and realistic role information.

- **1.7.2-M2 — D.** Career exploration combines motivation, capability evidence and role demands rather than guessing.

- **1.7.3-M1 — D.** A measurable development plan combines real practice, structured feedback and review of performance.

- **1.7.3-M2 — B.** A measurable plan requires applied practice, feedback and review against evidence.
